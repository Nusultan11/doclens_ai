# Deterministic offline chunking baseline v1

## Scope and decision

This report compares two offline character-based chunking strategies using the existing
structured extraction contract and the frozen Corpus v1. It does not evaluate embeddings,
retrieval, generation, or RAG quality and does not integrate chunking with the production API,
PostgreSQL, object storage, or a worker.

Decision: use the structure-aware strategy as the next offline baseline, with the fixed-size
strategy retained as the control. Both achieved complete normalized-content and provenance
coverage with no oversized chunks. Structure-aware chunking preserved the available structural
boundaries and reduced measured source-character duplication from 1.107657 to 1.027908, at the
cost of 42 additional chunks. This is an engineering/reproducibility decision, not a claim of
semantic or retrieval superiority.

## Corpus and reproduction

The local Corpus v1 contained 90 files. All 90 SHA-256 values matched the committed manifest, with
no missing or extra hashes: 30 PDF, 30 DOCX, and 30 XLSX. The primary evaluation used the default
extraction gate, so 56 `success` documents were chunked and 34 `needs_review` documents were
skipped explicitly for each strategy. No failed extraction entered chunking.

Configuration:

- chunking contract/version: `1.0`;
- maximum length: 1,200 characters;
- overlap: 120 characters;
- formula policy: formula text plus stored cached value, never execution;
- `needs_review`: skipped;
- maximum normalized source characters per document: 5,000,000;
- maximum chunks per document: 100,000.

Commands:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python -m doclens_ai.chunking `
  --input-dir local-corpus `
  --output-dir .eda-output\chunking-v1 `
  --max-characters 1200 `
  --overlap-characters 120

.\.venv\Scripts\python -m doclens_ai.chunking `
  --input-dir local-corpus `
  --output-dir .eda-output\chunking-v1-det-1 `
  --max-characters 1200 `
  --overlap-characters 120 `
  --omit-runtime-metrics
```

## Coverage and provenance methodology

The chunker first creates a deterministic normalized representation from extracted source units.
Line endings are normalized and outer whitespace is removed. PDF text blocks remain page-scoped;
DOCX paragraph/table-cell units retain block, table, row, column, and merge-span information; XLSX
units retain sheet, row, cell, merged-range, and table metadata locations. Formula text and stored
cached values are represented as data and never executed.

Normalized content coverage is the union of source-unit character ranges represented in chunks,
divided by the normalized source-unit character count for chunked documents. Inter-unit separator
characters generated by the chunker are excluded. Provenance coverage is the fraction of chunks
with at least one source span. Overlap increases emitted source characters but cannot increase
unique covered characters.

Every emitted chunk stayed within 1,200 characters, every normalized source character was
represented, and every chunk had provenance. The CLI persists only opaque identifiers, SHA-256,
configuration/fingerprints, counts, distributions, ratios, statuses, and latency. It does not
persist chunk text, extracted text, filenames, paths, worksheet names, or cell values.

## Strategy trade-offs

Fixed-size is a useful deterministic control with simple exact overlap. It produces dense chunks,
but may cross structural boundaries and duplicated 10.77% of unique source characters in this
run.

Structure-aware chunking never crosses PDF page or XLSX sheet scopes, keeps DOCX table content
separate from body paragraphs, and prefers whole table/worksheet rows. Oversized pages, rows, or
single cells are split deterministically with bounded overlap. Its shorter tail chunks explain the
lower mean length and slightly higher chunk count. The observed lower duplication is specific to
this corpus and configuration.

## Limitations and next evaluation

- Character counts are not tokenizer counts. Multilingual text and formula-heavy spreadsheets
  may have materially different token densities.
- The evaluation excludes all 34 `needs_review` extraction results by design; it does not show how
  missing OCR, PDF table structure, drawings, notes, or unsupported office content would affect
  downstream quality.
- XLSX values are stored OOXML values, not rendered display values. Missing formula caches and
  unavailable shared-formula definitions remain explicit limitations.
- Full coverage proves that the normalized representation was not silently dropped; it does not
  prove semantic completeness, good retrieval boundaries, or answer quality.
- SHA-256 and deterministic chunk fingerprints are internal reproducibility metadata, not content
  anonymization guarantees.

Before embeddings or indexing, evaluate both strategies with the selected tokenizer and a fixed,
source-grounded retrieval dataset. Compare retrieval hit/recall, citation provenance, ingestion
volume, and latency under identical conditions. Do not select a semantic chunker or add a
tokenizer dependency until those measurements demonstrate a concrete need.
